# Research informing the study

These notes distinguish published findings from choices made for this assignment. They are not claims that an existing study was reproduced.

1. **Barlacchi et al. (2015), Scientific Data 2, 150055.** [Publisher](https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2015.55). The dataset describes telecommunications activity on geographical grids and documents daily/weekly patterns and variation across locations. Internet activity is not a measurement in GB. This motivates temporal characterization and cross-area evaluation. The assignment's squares 4159 and 4556 must not be identified as the paper's Bocconi and Navigli examples: the paper names different square IDs (4259 and 4456). We do not assert land use for the assignment's squares.

2. **A. Azari, P. Papapetrou, S. Denic and G. Peters (2019), “Cellular Traffic Prediction and Classification: a comparative evaluation of LSTM and ARIMA.”** [arXiv:1906.00939](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00939). The study compares statistical and gated recurrent models on packet-level cellular data. Its findings depend on traffic regime, available features and training length; it does not establish that LSTM always wins. Its short packet-count intervals and feature-rich inputs differ from this assignment's aggregated 10-minute, single-area activity. It motivates retaining a simple statistical comparator and a persistence benchmark. Our regularized autoregression is not the paper's ARIMA: it has no moving-average error terms and shrinks a learned correction toward persistence.

3. **S. Bai, J. Z. Kolter and V. Koltun (2018), “An Empirical Evaluation of Generic Convolutional and Recurrent Networks for Sequence Modeling.”** [arXiv:1803.01271](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.01271). The paper motivates causal, dilated convolutions as an alternative to recurrence. Dilation expands the receptive field without recurrent state. Its benchmark tasks are not Milan mobile traffic, so its empirical rankings cannot be transferred directly. Our compact causal CNN uses this mechanism but omits the paper's full residual-block/weight-normalization architecture; it is not an exact TCN replication. For kernel size three, one convolution per dilation, receptive field equals 1 + 2 × sum(dilations).

4. **C. Zhang and P. Patras (2017), “Long-Term Mobile Traffic Forecasting Using Deep Spatio-Temporal Neural Networks.”** [arXiv:1712.08083](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.08083). This research exploits both spatial and temporal structure and targets longer forecasting horizons. It motivates a limitation and extension of our single-area, one-step study: neighboring-area inputs and longer-horizon prediction require separate investigation. Its published improvement percentages are not directly comparable with our metrics or setup.
